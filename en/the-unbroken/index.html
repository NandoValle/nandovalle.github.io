The Unbroken

Instrumental single by Nando Valle, 2:24, released on 11 September 2026. This page carries the measured sheet, the who the piece is dedicated to, and the originality check.

QUESTION. Does the track "The Unbroken" contain melodic quotation of a third party's protected repertoire?

ANSWER. None was detected. Three independent instruments were applied on 11 September 2026: a search for the longest identical interval sequence, pitch-class similarity with paired controls and transposition invariance, and YouTube's Content ID scan against the database of actual rights holders. None found third-party material. The longest passage shared with any reference is 6 notes, and it is an oscillating accompaniment figure. What each method does not prove is stated at the end.

The rule of this house is the usual one: the track ships with its method. Here the method had one extra question to answer, and the question came from the title.

Why it exists

The piece was written in honour of the artist's sister. That is the only thing this page says about it, and deliberately so: the rest is not public business. It is on record because it explains what the track is, and because a catalogue that publishes its method can also say, once, who a piece was written for.

The title, which is the interesting part

The piece was going to be called The Force. It did not reach release under that name, and the reason has nothing to do with music: The Force is a third party's registered trademark in entertainment. Releasing an original composition under the one name capable of attracting a trademark discussion is paying dearly for a single word.

With the title changed, one question remained that a site promising method cannot leave open: the provisional name pointed at a known repertoire. Does the music point there too? That question, and only that one, is what the measurement below answers.

The title was a trademark matter. The melody was a measurement matter. Different problems, handled as two.

The Unbroken says what the piece holds from start to finish, and says who it is dedicated to. It keeps the "The ___" cadence that motivated the old name and owes nothing to anyone. The variant containing the trademark inside the title was discarded on purpose: inserting words does not neutralise a trademark, because the test is confusion, not literal identity.

Method

None of the three checks was done by ear, and the first two use as references recordings from the artist's own archive plus classical and soundtrack controls, so that the result had something to be compared against. Environment: basic-pitch 0.4.0 and librosa 1.0.0.

1. Interval n-grams (catches local quotation)

Automatic transcription, top-line extraction by skyline, conversion to an interval sequence (which makes the comparison immune to transposition) and a search for the longest identical contiguous subsequence. This is the method that catches the classic case: two identical bars in the middle of a different piece.

2. CENS chroma with paired controls

Cosine similarity between pitch-class profiles, 6 s window. Here is the part worth more than the result: without correcting for key, the references appeared to win comfortably (86.2% of frames). With key corrected, the advantage fell to +0.005 and 55.9% of frames, which is a coin toss. Transposed to the right key, Beethoven's Fifth scores 0.824, inside the same band as the references. All the apparent resemblance was E minor.

This is the kind of finding that only surfaces when the control is measured alongside. The first version of the number looked strong and was wrong.

3. YouTube Content ID

The only one of the three that does not depend on a calculation of ours: it runs the real audio against the database of the real rights holders. Unlisted video, audio verified before upload (0.999964 correlation with the master, to make sure the encoding had not altered the fingerprint).

The Content ID result deserves an extra line, because it is counter-intuitive: a video with no claim at all would have been a worse result. A blank result is ambiguous, it may only mean the scan has not run yet. Here exactly one claim appeared, issued by the artist's own distribution chain, covering 0:00 to 2:24, the entire track. That proves the system compared the complete audio against the database. Overlapping claims from different holders are routine in Content ID: had any segment matched a third party's asset, it would have appeared as a second row on the same screen. None did.

Reproducing, and what this page does not publish

What goes online is the material belonging to the track itself:

  • alvo_unbroken.mid: the automatic transcription of the track, the file the interval search ran on. Anyone wanting to redo the count starts here.

And here comes the caveat a method page is obliged to give, even when it is uncomfortable. Three things were deliberately left out, and each one costs reproducibility:

  • The reference MIDIs. Some of them are transcriptions of recordings of third-party work. Publishing a transcription of protected work in order to prove there is no derivative material would distribute derivative material on the way.
  • The scripts, as they were used. They read paths from the artist's personal archive, with folder and file names that have nothing to do with whoever reads this page. Publishing them raw would expose the layout of a private disk.
  • The names of the works used as references. They are in the internal measurement file, not here. What this page asserts is the result: no third-party passage was found. Naming the repertoire tested would turn a preventive check into an insinuation of kinship that the measurement itself does not support.

Honest consequence of the three: this page is not reproducible end to end by a third party. It is auditable in what it publishes, the track's MIDI and the rule described above, and not in the rest. Anyone with a legitimate interest in checking the full comparison can request the complete package through the site's contact.

Honest caveats

A report that does not declare its own limits is not a report, it is advertising. These are this measurement's, and the third is the most important:

  • Chroma measures pitch class, not an isolated melody. A quotation hidden in an inner voice, under dense texture, can escape.
  • Automatic transcription of dense orchestral material is noisy. Absence of a finding, in material like this, is weak evidence on its own. The conclusion rests on three instruments converging, not on one number.
  • The references in the first two tests are proxies. GAP: it was not confirmed that any of them contains specifically the piece the old title evoked. If the starting material were that one and no reference contained it, those two tests would not see it. That is precisely why Content ID was run: it does not depend on our references.
  • Content ID matches recordings above all. It closes the hypothesis of reproducing a protected master. It is not legal clearance on composition, and its database does not cover all existing repertoire.

Summing up: the audio does not support a derivative work, by three different routes, and none of the three proves absolute originality. That is what the measurement gives. The rest would be assertion without measure, which is exactly what this house does not publish.
