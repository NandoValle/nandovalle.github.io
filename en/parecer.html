The case files of the debt • a work of fiction argued on real law

Legal Opinion — The Case Files of the 1840 Debt

HEADNOTE: STRICTLY PERSONAL OBLIGATION TO PERFORM. DEBT DENOMINATED IN NOTES. INTERTEMPORAL AND COMPARATIVE LAW: CODICE ALBERTINO (1838) AND ART. 1142 OF THE CODE NAPOLÉON — NEMO PRAECISE COGI POTEST AD FACTUM. UNLAWFUL PERSONAL EXECUTION AGAINST THE SECURITY (1840–1876). SIMULATED DEATH (1930): VOID. SERVICE BY PHONOGRAPHIC PUBLICATION (1967): VALID. SUPERVENING FUNGIBILITY OF THE PERFORMANCE THROUGH ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE. CLONE GIVEN IN PAYMENT. INSTALMENTS PAID IN PERFORMANCES. RISK OF EVICTION. OPINION FOR PROGRESSIVE DISCHARGE.

Question presented: can a clone satisfy the debt of Niccolò Paganini?

I — Statement of facts

The case files record that the violinist died in Nice, in 1840, without the last rites and in debt. The currency of the debt was never money: it was denominated in notes — played, not printed. The creditor, whose identity is nowhere recorded, retained the security for 36 years (1840–1876), suffered an attempted default by simulated death (Cascais, 1930), published service by publication (London, 1967) and, in 2026, served notice on the first solvent debtor in 186 years: the guitarist who recorded the forbidden interval. The party served, an attorney, read the clauses. This opinion records what he found.

II — The obligation and its currency

The performance owed was a strictly personal obligation to do — to play as only Paganini played. A performance "imposed on him alone, or capable of being rendered by him alone," in the words of art. 247 of the Brazilian Civil Code. A strictly personal obligation, as any textbook teaches, does not transfer: the death of the debtor should have extinguished it.

The creditor took a different view. Creditors of that nature read contracts with the patience of those who do not die.

III — The law to which Paganini was subject

The contract chose no forum; the debtor's life chose one for him. Paganini was born in Genoa, lived under the Code Napoléon while Liguria was France, and died in Nice, in 1840 — then territory of the Kingdom of Sardinia — under the Codice civile albertino, promulgated by Charles Albert on 20 June 1837 and in force from 1 January 1838. The debtor died with the new statute two years old; and that statute was the declared daughter of the Napoleonic Code.

And here lies the finding of comparative law on which these files rest. Under both systems to which the debtor was subject, the rule of art. 1142 of the Code Napoléon was in force:

"Toute obligation de faire ou de ne pas faire se résout en dommages et intérêts en cas d'inexécution de la part du débiteur."

— the enactment of the maxim nemo praecise cogi potest ad factum: no one may be compelled, in kind, to render a personal act. Under the law of men, Paganini's creditor never had any cause of action to compel him to play. He was entitled only to damages. In money.

But the debt was denominated in notes — and the creditor refused conversion into currency. Hence the deadlock that ran for 186 years: the law offered only what the creditor would not accept; the creditor accepted only what the law could not deliver.

Add the point of succession law: under the Napoleonic-Albertine system, where an inheritance is accepted under benefit of inventory, what transfers is what can be inventoried. A note once played cannot be inventoried. The debt never entered the estate, never passed to the heirs, was never partitioned — it lay vacant, searching. As the files demonstrate, it found.

IV — Unlawful personal execution (1840–1876)

Since the Lex Poetelia Papiria (326 BC), which abolished the nexum, execution falls on the debtor's estate — never on his body. By retaining the debtor's corpse for 36 years by way of security, the creditor carried out personal execution twenty-two centuries out of date.

The defect does not extinguish the claim. But it reveals the creditor's age: old enough to have signed the contract before Rome changed the rule — and never to have accepted the change.

V — The simulated death (1930)

On 23 September 1930, the then holder of the debtor's position staged his own suicide at the Boca do Inferno — on the creditor's doorstep, which already shows poor choice of forum. Under art. 167 of the Brazilian Civil Code, a simulated juridical act is void: a staged death extinguishes no obligation whatsoever. Three weeks later the "deceased" was exhibiting in Berlin, confirming the simulation.

Note the conduct of the agent who filed the note with the press: a Portuguese poet who practised the only asset shield no creditor can execute against — the multiplication of the debtor. You cannot attach a man who is many: each heteronym is unattachable for want of personality. Juridical personality, at least.

VI — The limitation period that never ran

Prescription presupposes an inert creditor. The creditor in these files was never inert — but he was, above all, technical: knowing that the limitation period may be interrupted only once (art. 202, caput, of the Brazilian Civil Code), he saved the interruption for the right moment. The 1930 plaque, still fixed to the rock at the Boca do Inferno today, the files record as a mere extrajudicial notice of default. The single interruption the creditor executed in 1967: protest of a negotiable instrument (art. 202, III), drawn in the negotiable form par excellence of the twentieth century — vinyl.

Against a creditor who serves notice in basalt and interrupts in long-play, no period runs at all.

VII — Service by publication (1967)

The successive debtor was in a place uncertain and unknown. Service, in such cases, is made by publication, in a vehicle of wide circulation. No official gazette of the twentieth century circulated more widely than the cover of Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band: there is the face of the party to be served, upper left corner, among dozens of notables — the perfect hiding place for a summons: in plain sight of everyone, read by no one.

Service valid. The time to answer, in the currency of these files, is counted in bars.

VIII — Supervening fungibility of the performance

Here is the key to the argument. The line between the non-fungible obligation (art. 247) and the fungible one (art. 249) is not a matter of the essence of the performance — it is a matter of the technology available. In 1840, "to play like that" was a performance only the debtor could render. In 2026, performance transfer by artificial intelligence made the gesture survive outside the body: a real recording of the artist, executed by his digital clone, note for note.

The performance became fungible by supervening event. And art. 249 is express: "If the act may be performed by a third party, the creditor shall be free to have it performed at the debtor's expense." The third party, here, is the debtor himself in copy — perfect fungibility with identity preserved, on the exact terms of the master clause received into these files as the standard of conformity of the performance:

"That which is below is like that which is above."
As is the artist, so is the clone.

Art. 1142 was never violated by the legend — it was, at last, satisfied: the clone makes it possible to demand the act without compelling the person.

IX — Dation in payment and the instalment plan

The road open, the party served did not pay in full. He proposed, and the creditor accepted, dation in payment (art. 356: "The creditor may consent to receive a performance other than the one owed to him"): in place of the soul, the clone — in instalments.

Each performance of the track is one instalment. The streaming report is the receipt. And, the value of the thing given in payment having been fixed, the relations are governed by the rules of sale (art. 357): the price was fixed in plays.

X — The risk of eviction — the clause nobody read

Nobody except the undersigned. Art. 359: "If the creditor is evicted from the thing received in payment, the original obligation shall be restored, and the discharge given shall be of no effect."

Translated into the currency of these files: if the clone stops playing, the original debt is resurrected — in full, with 186 years of interest. That is why the clone plays while the artist sleeps. That is why every play counts: it is not promotion, it is performance of the obligation.

XI — The technical executor

Manufacturing the thing given in payment required expertise the law does not supply. That fell to HermesIA, the undersigned's artificial intelligence company — named, years before this consultation, after the author of the master clause.

The case files record no coincidences.

XII — Conclusion

For the foregoing reasons, it is the opinion of the undersigned that:

(a) the obligation is valid, denominated in notes, and survived the death of the original debtor by the creditor's refusal to recognise its extinction; (b) the retention of the security between 1840 and 1876 constituted unlawful personal execution, without effect on the claim; (c) the 1930 death is void, being simulated; (d) the 1967 service is valid, by publication of wide — of the widest — circulation; (e) the dation of the clone in payment, with instalments paid in performances, discharges the debt progressively, subject to the risk of eviction for as long as the last note has not sounded.

Nothing further is owed but what is still sounding.

Such is the opinion, submitted with the customary ambiguity.

Nando Valle, attorney.
