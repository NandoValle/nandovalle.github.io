IX · the measurement · August 2026

In August 2026, Valle recorded Insane Baroque Blaze — a baroque piece on the guitar. Weeks later he went to measure his own recording, out of technical curiosity.

He was sure of one thing: that there was no tritone in it at all, the interval historically nicknamed diabolus in musica.

He was wrong, and wrong on the basics.

The tritone lives inside every dominant seventh chord. Whoever writes baroque meets tritones, knowingly or not. The count was never going to be zero. And a caveat is due: there was no universal church decree “banning” the interval; the excommunication story is later folklore.

In the full passes, the two independent engines returned 83, 91 and 80 melodic tritones. A third Basic Pitch pass, tuned to the low register, returned 49. That is why this page does not treat “80 to 90” as a universal range: it gives the four results separately. The first reported occurrence appears at 0:01.5 — C#4 to G4, right on the opening leap.

Then somebody tried to reproduce the count from the published description alone, and could not. Nine variants of the rule were tested; the divergence ran from four to nineteen occurrences depending on the file.

The defect was not in the numbers. It was in their description.

⚠ correction published on 25 August 2026

The report said “an interval of 6 semitones” for the melodic line. The detector had always counted interval class 6 — the tritone at any octave — exactly as the harmonic rule already stated.

Change the phrase and the note count, the melodic tritones, the harmonic tritones and the first occurrence all reproduce the files again.

One wrong word in the description of the method.
The numbers were always right.

The corrected rule

  • Melodic: two consecutive notes with interval class 6; the next note begins within 0.6 s of the end of the previous one. Consecutive, here: beginning more than 0.02 s after the previous onset — below that the note counts as simultaneous — and only the first melodic event following each note.
  • Harmonic: two notes sounding together for at least 30 ms with interval class 6.
  • Control: the bass, by the same method, returns 2 melodic and 0 harmonic.
Not: “take my word for it”.

But: here is the rule. Here are the numbers. Here are the files. Do it again.

X · the distance

The file does not force a conclusion.

Paganini never declared any pact about himself. He denied it in writing, twice. The ones who declared were the audience.

The word “devil” did not come from him. It came from people who had no other word for what they were seeing.

Paganini died in 1840. Crowley published Aha! in 1910. Insane Baroque Blaze was released in 2026. There is no document linking the three events.

Object 01

A man.

Turned into legend by people trying to explain a technique that looked impossible.

Object 02

A text.

On will, experience, silence, seven keys — and the test of experiment.

Object 03

A recording.

Public, audible, measured by independent transcriptions and shipped with the files for reproduction.

Perhaps this means nothing. That possibility stays open. Perhaps someone else will find another explanation. Better still: the method lets them try.

Whatever is left after all the documents will not be the work of this text. It will be the work of what stays unexplained — and of what each reader decides to project onto it.

Questions this page provokes.

        

          
Did Nando Valle sell his soul to the devil?

          
No — and the essay does not claim Paganini did either.

          

            See the full answer
            
Paganini denied the rumours about himself in his own lifetime, in writing. This page is about something else: how a historical reputation was built, which documents survived, and what happens when fact, interpretation and imagination are set side by side without pretending to be the same thing.

          

        

        

          
Does the site claim a supernatural link between Paganini, Crowley and the music?

          
No. The link built here is narrative, not historical.

          

            See the full answer
            
Paganini supplies the case; Crowley supplies another language for a few questions — will, experience and silence; the recording supplies the part that can actually be tested.

          

        

        

          
Why does Aleister Crowley appear here?

          
Because Aha!, published in 1910, holds three ideas that run through the project. None of them proves anything — they frame the question.

          

            See the full answer
            
Because Aha!, published in 1910, holds three ideas that run through the project in an unusual way: the paradox of seven keys and eight stages; the image of the soul struck by the “plectron of the Will”; and the demand that doctrine be submitted to the test of experiment. None of them proves anything about Paganini or about this music. They frame the question.

          

        

        

          
Does Insane Baroque Blaze really contain tritones?

          
Yes: 83, 91, 49 and 80 melodic tritones across four published transcriptions. And that is less extraordinary than “diabolus” suggests.

          

            See the full answer
            
Four published guitar transcriptions return 83, 91, 49 and 80 melodic tritones, given per file and per engine; the first reported occurrence is at roughly 0:01.5. As a control, the bass line by the same method returns 2 melodic and 0 harmonic. The report carries the musical context, the metric used and the corrections — and the point is that the method, the detector and the files were published so anyone can check.

          

        

        

          
Was the video made with artificial intelligence?

          
In part: the guitar performance is real, by Nando Valle; the image is synthetic.

          

            See the full answer
            
The original musical performance is Nando Valle on guitar. In the video, a digital clone reproduces that performance from the real recording with the help of artificial intelligence. The image is synthetic; the performance behind it is not.
