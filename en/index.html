The hidden instrument

Between 1840 and 1876, the body of Niccolò Paganini was buried and disinterred nine times. That is not legend: it is record. Nando Valle's essay starts from that anomaly and works through the documents that explain it — the refusal Paganini himself made, the reputation Europe gave him, the medical record of what it cost him, the two denials he published, and the instrument he kept out of sight. Every claim below has a source, and the caveats are stated.

1801–1804 — the hidden instrument
Paganini drops off the map and spends the time studying the guitar — not the electric guitar, which would not exist for until the 1930s, but the six-string guitar all of Europe played. He writes forty-three Ghiribizzi, thirty-seven sonatas and another two dozen pieces for solo guitar alone. And he almost never plays it in public.
1823 — the price
Treatment with mercury begins, in enormous doses combined with opium. He loses every tooth; the jaw rots from within. By July 1839 he has not a single tooth left and can barely eat. Modern analysis of his hair, by mass spectrometry, still finds the metal in the strands.
1828 and 1831 — the denials
Against the rumour that he was the Devil's son, Paganini publishes a letter from his own mother in a Prague newspaper. Three years later, in the Revue musicale, he publishes another about the Vienna spectator who swore he saw the Devil guiding his bow, calling the episode ridiculous. And he goes on arriving dressed in black, in a dark carriage.
1837 and 1838 — the casino, the will, the signed guitar
He lends his name to a gambling house in Paris; it fails within months and he sells off most of his instruments. That same year he leaves the Cannone to the City of Genoa by will, to be perpetually preserved — the violin is still there, taken out of its case once a month for someone to play it. In 1838, on his last visit to Paris, he signs his own name on the soundboard of a Grobert guitar. Berlioz would sign the other side. The object is at the Musée de la Musique, item E.375.
1840 — the refusal
In May the Bishop of Nice sends a priest to administer the last rites. Paganini refuses: he believes there is still time. A week later he is dead, with no voice left to call anyone. The priest reports the refusal to the bishop, and on that report the Church denies him consecrated ground. With the reputation he had, no one in Europe protests.
1840–1876 — the nine burials
Embalmed, the body spends more than a year in the cellar of the house where he died, ends up in an olive-oil factory, and passes thirty-six years with no permanent grave, buried and disinterred nine times. Only in 1876 does a pope authorise the burial.
2026 — the measurement
Nando Valle records "Insane Baroque Blaze" and then goes to measure his own recording. Two independent transcription engines find between eighty and ninety tritones in the guitar line — the interval the Middle Ages nicknamed diabolus in musica. The first sits at second one-and-a-half. The report is published, with method, numbers and caveats — and it can be reproduced or refuted.

The essay draws no conclusion about what any of it means. It sets the documents side by side and stops. Whatever remains after that is not the work of the text.

Frequently asked questions

Did Nando Valle sell his soul to the devil?

No. And there was no pact in Paganini's case either — he himself denied it in print twice, in 1828 and in 1831. What Valle publishes is not a legend: it is a documented essay, with a source for every historical claim and the caveats stated in the apparatus. Where there is belief rather than fact, the text says it is belief.

Does Insane Baroque Blaze really contain the tritone, the diabolus in musica?

Yes — and the number is not the interesting part. Measurement over automatic transcription, using two independent engines, finds between 80 and 90 melodic tritones in the guitar line. The first sits at 0:01.5 — C#4 leaping to G4, the tonic going straight to the forbidden interval in the very first gesture of the track. But a tritone is unavoidable in baroque writing: one lives inside every dominant seventh chord, and the track is in C# harmonic minor, a scale that carries structural pairs. Whoever writes baroque writes tritones, knowingly or not. What remains is where the first one sits.
